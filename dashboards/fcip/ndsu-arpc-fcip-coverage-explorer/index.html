The FCIP Crop Production Coverage Explorer

Of each dollar of U.S. crop production value, how much stands behind federal crop insurance, and where the rest sits

One dollar, eleven places it can be

Take a year's crop production value at the farm gate, as the USDA Economic Research Service (ERS) publishes it, nationally and for every state. Then ask where each dollar sits with respect to the Federal Crop Insurance Program.

A dollar is not simply insured or not. It can be inside a basic guarantee, inside a supplemental buy-up such as SCO or ECO, in a deductible band a product could have filled but nobody bought (a coverage gap), in the part of the deductible no product reaches, or on acres that carry no policy at all.

The explorer splits every year's crop value across those eleven layers, so the shares always add to one.

How to read the Coverage tab

The bar at the top picks the slice: a place (the nation, any states, or a region), the units, and the years. The right handle of the year slider is the focus year; the left handle is where the history starts.

Reading, on the left, says in words what the figure shows and flags anything that should stop you quoting a number. Panel A is the focus year's stack, panel B the whole history with years outside the window faded.

The map shows one layer across the states, in dollars or as a share, with your selection outlined; click a state to select it. Growth by band compares the focus year with the year before, the five years before, and the start year.
